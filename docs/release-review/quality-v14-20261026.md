## Websites v2 — the owner follow-up

### Diagnosis (offline, before any paid call)
The phase 2 comps were faithful to what the designer asked for, and the builds followed the comps. The gap to the references was upstream and in what the gates measured:
1. **Muted palettes.** The designer chose cream, beige and dusty grounds; the references each carry one saturated field (blue, yellow, forest, black) at full strength.
2. **The display face drifted.** The build picked a wider, lighter or different family than the comp drew (w22 extended grotesk; w01 v2 a light serif where the comp was bold). Nothing measured weight.
3. **Too few comps.** The agent asked for four once and then let the run go on; and the comp tool refused section comps that had no navigation (they have no header), so every run lost two calls and w05 lost its section comps.
4. **The gate measured scale and image area only.** It did not check the ground colour or the weight.

### Fixes (feature/image-led-sites)
- `f58ea4c` — the designer prompt gains a "signature colour and scale" section (one confident field, flat, at full strength; headline at poster scale); the build must take the library face closest to the comp's type words, never wider, lighter or of another family, and the comp's exact ground and accent hex. The comp prompt draws colours flat like a printed poster. `compPlan` names the comps: first screen desktop and phone, then the next two content sections (not forms or quotes). The comp gate asks up to twice for the missing comps while they are affordable. Comps record their ground; the fidelity gate measures the ground behind the biggest headline (`ground`, RGB distance over 90 fails).
- `8d90aaa` — section comps are drawn without the header and need no navigation; the type words lose a doubled "huge"; heavy type is the default unless the words say light, thin or hairline; the fidelity gate fails a heavy comp built under font-weight 600 (`weight`). Tests: check-layout-probe 9, check-comp-prompt 8, check-image-vision 15, check-concept 6; builder run-checks 31/31.

### Honest verdict: what a real website can match from one generated image
Decks and books ship the generated image itself, so they can match the references completely. A website ships live, responsive, accessible code, and the comp is only the target. What carries over well, and does now: the ground and accent colours, the type scale and weight, the split and image area, the photograph's subject and light, the pill and tag shapes, the section rhythm, and the phone first screen. What a single generated image cannot give a real site:
1. **Generated lettering.** Kazu's brush wordmark, Nonna Rosa's drawn serif and r_cat's hand lettering are drawn by the image model. Live headlines are limited to the font library (they must stay editable, translatable, searchable and readable by screen readers). A logo can be an image wordmark with alt text; a headline should not be.
2. **The exact display face.** Comps draw an unnamed face; the build takes the nearest library font. The closer the library (heavy condensed sans, high-contrast serif, brush display), the closer the match.
3. **Type and photograph as one picture.** The references shoot the product on the field colour itself (r_bikes's bike on blue) or set type over the photograph. The built site places a separately generated photograph in its own box; the comp cannot be reused as the photograph because its text is baked in.
4. **One width.** A comp is 2560x1440; the site reflows from 360 to 2560 px, so line breaks and measure differ (the three and four line headlines above).
5. **Invented facts.** Comps can invent small facts (in phase 2, w22's comp said Mon–Fri where the brief said Tue–Fri); the build follows the brief, and missing prices show as "[Price]" until the owner gives them.

What closes most of the remaining gap, in order: (a) a line-count gate (headline lines at 1440 px no more than the comp's, with balanced wrapping and clamp sizes); (b) the hero photograph prompted from the comp's reading (subject, angle, background on the exact ground hex for cutout shots) and checked against the comp by vision; (c) a wider display library with a font-matching step; (d) carrying devices such as the reversed highlight block into the fidelity check; (e) an image wordmark option for logos; (f) real owner content and prices. With these, first screens should match the references as closely as w22 and w01 v3 do now on every run, not most runs; hand-drawn lettering remains image-only.

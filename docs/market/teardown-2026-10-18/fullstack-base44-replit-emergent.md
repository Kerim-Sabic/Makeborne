# AI full-stack builders teardown: Base44, Replit Agent, Emergent (as of 2026-10-18)

Scope: how each product works and why its output/experience is good or bad. Pricing tables are deliberately omitted (covered in the 13 Oct 2026 table). Every non-obvious claim carries a URL. Where only third-party or competitor sources exist, that is flagged.

---

## 1. Base44 (Wix)

### 1.1 End-to-end flow

**Landing → first prompt.** Home page prompt box; you can type, dictate (Speech to text), attach images/PDFs (visual reference), spreadsheets/CSV (read for columns and sample data), Word/Markdown (content and structure), or use `+` → *Start from URL* (Content & Design / Design only, credits charged, you must own the site; "private backend code, hidden data, and third-party integrations are not copied") or *Import from Figma* (launched 15 Apr 2026, frames can be added as pages to an existing app since 30 Jun 2026, 300 MB limit). Source: https://docs.base44.com/Getting-Started/starting-from-your-first-prompt.md and https://docs.base44.com/changelog/product.

**Clarifying questions and Plan mode.** Since 5 Oct 2026 "the AI asks clarifying questions before a first build", one question at a time, each answerable, skippable, or filled with *Use a placeholder*. Plan mode (launched 5 Feb 2026) is a separate dropdown option: a freeform conversation (since 10 Sep 2026) with a four-section progress tracker (Intent and goal, Audience and roles, Core flows, Design); questions arrive as option cards, and since 2 Aug 2026 are batched into "one or two compact cards of up to three questions each" rather than single-question rounds. The Design step shows 4 mockups of the main screen, each refinable up to 3 times. Plan mode costs "only a fraction of a credit per message"; the first build after *Start Building* uses ~1 credit; a plan conversation holds up to 50 messages. Sources: docs above and changelog.

**Generation.** Third parties describe a real-time build log (schema → frontend → auth → deploy) and "usually 2-5 minutes for the initial build"; the result is "a real deployed app, not a static preview" at a live URL (https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/what-is-base44, author sells Base44 rescue services). Since 6 Oct 2026 you can "steer an app's first build with Send now" (i.e. inject mid-build instructions).

**Editing.** Three chat modes (https://docs.base44.com/Building-your-app/AI-chat-modes.md):
- *Build* (default): "Acts instantly on your prompts."
- *Discuss*: "no code changes until you approve them"; runs on its own cheap model (GPT 6 Luna per 4 Oct 2026 changelog) at "~0.3 message credits" per message. Toggle with Cmd/Ctrl+`.`.
- *Edit* (visual): select one element or a group of repeating elements in the preview; change colours/text/font/spacing from a toolbar, open *Edit Tailwind* or *Code*, *Delete*, or *Edit Element* to describe an AI change. Manual edits are free; AI element edits cost message credits. Undo/redo "up to 50 steps within the same visual edit session"; rebuilt with auto-save 31 Mar 2026.
- Model picker: Auto by default; Builder plan and above can pick (Oct 2026 picker includes Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, GPT 6 Sol/Luna/Astra, Gemini 4 Argon, Haiku 5.5, Fable 5.1, Base 1). Since 13 Oct 2026 there are "Effort levels … Low for quick changes up to Max for the hardest problems". Since 30 Aug 2026 you can have "two models build the same message side by side" (2x credits).
- Versions: "Version history is renamed Backup & Restore" (17 Sep 2026); compare any two versions (14 Sep 2026); security-scan fixes are "recorded in AI chat with a checkpoint beforehand" (2 Jul 2026). Branches (since 5 Aug 2026) are "full copies with their own previews" with separate build queues; protected main branches route changes to a branch; revert works on branches.
- Code: a Code tab (saves to current branch), GitHub sync (workspace-level since 27 Sep 2026; AI resolves sync conflicts since 11 Aug 2026). Important caveat: GitHub export covers the frontend; "the backend remains locked within Base44's infrastructure" (https://www.willo.ai/blog/base44-review, competitor) and "the integrated database is the main lock-in" (justinmckelvey).

**Publish.** One-click publish to a Base44 subdomain; custom domains now included in Starter (11 Aug 2026), automatic DNS for owned domains (19 Aug 2026), free first-year domain on yearly plans (4 Oct 2026). Visibility options include public, "Public with login", private; embedding can be restricted "to anyone, listed sites, or no one" (1 Oct 2026). Auth is built in (email, social, OIDC from Elite, SSO on Business/Enterprise). Payments: "Base Payments powered by Wix" documented 17 Feb 2026, recurring subscriptions 14 Apr 2026, UK 30 Apr 2026, Payouts tab 6 Oct 2026; also "One-click Stripe setup creates the API key for the user" (13 Jul 2026) and a Stripe Subscriptions tab (29 Sep 2026). Mobile: store submission from the editor generating IPA/AAB files (3 Feb 2026) — but Trustpilot reviewers note the output is a webview wrapper that lacks some native functions (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/base44.com).

**Sharing/collaboration.** Workspaces with pooled credits (16 Mar 2026: "All credits are pooled between people in the workspace"); invite collaborators from the editor search box (10 Oct 2026); comments pinned to app elements (31 Aug 2026); branch comments. Partner Program (https://docs.base44.com/Community-and-support/partner-program): joinable at Maker (Level 2) or via agency sponsorship on Pro+; tools include "a unified view of apps across client workspaces", app transfer to a client workspace, "credit delegation" (spend your credits in a client's workspace), client conversation threads with an auto-answering agent, private branches, and a public Partner Directory with hourly rate, "Starting from $X", response time and reviews. Partners can "request card payments in client threads (US only)" since 7 Sep 2026. Transfers to external owners are for Enterprise plans and approved Partners; the recipient has 7 days to accept.

### 1.2 Architecture and generation technique

- **Vertically integrated backend is the thesis.** Shlomo on 20VC: "The whole reason for Base44's existence is the built in back end … for every application you already have a built in database, user management, authentication, integrations, analytics" (https://www.996.fm/episodes/20vc--ep-05bb4694/). Entities (tables with RLS; "Row-level security denies access when a user value is empty" since 2 Sep 2026), backend functions (Deno-style serverless), Core integrations (invokeLLM, SendEmail, file upload, image gen) metered in *integration credits* separate from *message credits*.
- **Model routing.** Auto mode routes "smaller visual changes" to faster models and "structural or logic-heavy changes" to deeper reasoning models (third-party: https://www.willo.ai/blog/base44-review). Since 29 Jun 2026 **Base 1**, "Base44's first in-house model, trained on real building patterns from across the platform", sits in the picker. Wix's Q2 2026 release calls Base44 the "first app-creation platform to launch proprietary LLM" and credits it for taking Base44 from "near-zero non-GAAP gross margin entering the year" to an expected ~60% in 2H 2026 (https://www.wix.com/press-room/home/post/wix-reports-second-quarter-2026-results). Abrahami: "continuous training based on what works best for our users". Implication: in-house models are being used first for cheap/common turns (Discuss runs on a cheap model; Superagent automations "default to a faster model"; GLM 5.2 became the default Superagent model 1 Aug 2026), not to replace frontier models on hard builds.
- **Consistency of UI.** Design systems: generated "from a website URL" (21 Jul 2026) or from "a brand description … a complete, animated design system" (28 Jul 2026), from published npm packages (3 Aug 2026), applied to captured or imported apps; only owners/admins manage design systems (5 Oct 2026). Redesigned component library with search/categories/previews (8 Oct 2026). Wix font catalogue in pickers (7 Sep 2026). Output is Tailwind-based React SPA; Visual Edit exposes *Edit Tailwind*.
- **SPA-only architecture** is a known weakness: "Base44 generates SPAs by default, and as of March 2026 no SSR or pre-rendering option exists" (https://muratesmer.com/blog/base44-review-2026/). Partial mitigations shipped: SEO & GEO tab (26 Apr 2026) with llms.txt, per-route meta/breadcrumbs, "richer HTML snapshots for crawlers" (10 Jun 2026), per-page indexing controls, AI Visibility tab; SSR only for builders' public profiles (20 Jul 2026), not published apps (https://www.base44devs.com/blog/base44-after-wix-acquisition-what-changed).
- **Security tooling** post-Wiz: Wiz found (Jul 2025) undocumented register/verify-OTP endpoints that accepted only a non-secret `app_id`, bypassing all auth including SSO; patched within 24 hours (https://thehackernews.com/2025/07/wiz-uncovers-critical-access-bypass.html). Since then: security scans with one-click fixes for "permission issues, exposed secrets, and unsecured backend functions" (14 Apr 2026), code vulnerability scanning (6 Jul 2026), a "credit protection check" (4 Sep 2026), pinning an app requires a current scan (4 Oct 2026), portfolio-wide Enterprise Security Center (2 Aug 2026).
- **Run bounding and cost control.** No long autonomous runs for apps; each message is a bounded turn and "the exact count is calculated after each message runs" (i.e. cost shown after, not before). Superagent turns can run "up to 20 minutes" (23 Sep 2026). Failed Superagent browser actions are not charged (26 Jul 2026). Credit usage page split by members/apps/Superagents/sources (12 Oct 2026). "Auto approve still asks before widening data access" (30 Sep 2026).
- **Base Code** (launched 28 Sep 2026): connect a GitHub repo; "Base Code's agent learns the codebase and configures a cloud environment for it" incl. databases and sample data; non-engineers edit via chat and ship PRs; reads SKILL.md; AI reads PR check status and merge readiness; "the first Base Code turn is free"; "upstream changes are tested in a sandbox before reaching main" (https://www.wix.com/press-room/home/post/base44-launches-base-code-helping-teams-collaborate-and-ship-faster). A security write-up warns Base44's terms treat connected code as customer data under a broad licence that includes training (https://greenlitbooks.com/field-notes/is-base-code-safe).
- **Superagents** (11 Mar 2026): cross-tool personal agents built in 4 steps (goal, connect tools, add files/memory/context, workflows), scheduled or triggered workflows, memory that tracks "goals, decisions, and blockers per conversation", channels (WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage, Slack, LINE, phone number from Builder plan), Chrome extension, subagents for background work (https://docs.base44.com/Getting-Started/superagent).

### 1.3 Onboarding and activation

- Free plan: 25 message credits/month capped at 5/day (was 7/day in early 2025), 100 integration credits (was 500), up to 5 apps; no card required. Reviewers: "most users hit the free ceiling within 1-2 days of building" (https://www.banani.co/blog/base44-ai-review).
- Time-to-first-app: 2–5 minutes to a live URL. Templates are free for new listings (1 Sep 2026); a Prompt library and Prompt guide are offered in the first-prompt flow.
- Credit explanation before spending: weak. Discuss and Plan are explicitly cheap; Build cost is only known after the turn. Since 24 Sep 2026 "the AI chat can answer questions about your plan, credits, and access". Since 30 Jul 2026 users over the limit can still write messages but not send.
- Activation lesson from the founder: Lenny's Podcast lists "the counterintuitive product decision that tripled activation by removing a 'helpful' feature"; a summary indicates Base44 "initially aimed to guide users through generating user flows before app implementation" — i.e. an up-front planning step was removed to let people see an app immediately (https://podpulse.ai/podcast/lennys-podcast-product-growth-career/lennys-podcast-product-growth-career-solo-founder-80m-exit-6-months-the-base44-bootstrapped-startup-success-story-maor-shlomo). Notably Plan mode came back in 2026 as *optional*.

### 1.4 Quality mechanisms and weaknesses

What makes output good: complete first result (working DB, auth, deployed URL) rather than pretty; in a side-by-side "Lovable's version looked more polished … Base44's was subjectively not that good-looking, but it had a working database with no extra setup" (banani). What makes it generic: "functional but generic", "layouts feel boxed in"; login screens show Base44 branding and "can't be fully customized" — "unprofessional for client-facing apps" (willo).

Failure modes: "Multi-step workflows, conditional business rules and edge-case handling frequently break down"; "Base44 will fight you" on intricate logic; platform-level bugs (July 2025 function routing bug "broke backend logic across multiple apps … with no immediate fix communicated"; June 2025 backend function/image upload failures) "could not be diagnosed or fixed from within the project" (muratesmer). Outage 3 Feb 2026 "exposed the absence of SLA contracts outside of enterprise tiers" (justinmckelvey).

Most common 2026 complaints (Trustpilot 2.8/5, 915 reviews, 54% 1-star — https://www.trustpilot.com/review/base44.com; G2 — https://www.g2.com/products/base44):
1. Credits burned on the AI's own mistakes and loops: "software that consumes credits without completing tasks properly"; a platform error "consumed 16,384 integration credits" and support restored only part.
2. Credits don't roll over and can't be topped up without upgrading a tier.
3. "AI deleted files after being told not to"; it "would alter the entire design of a website when only minor changes were intended".
4. Context loss between sessions: G2 headline "A powerful tool with amnesia and ADHD, billed by the message".
5. Support slow/automated, ~2-day replies; cancellation/downgrade friction, charges after cancelling, "No refunds … 'its in our user agreement'".
6. Site taken offline when plan lapsed; a German reviewer recovered by copying code out and rebuilding elsewhere.
7. Mobile output is a webview, not native.

### 1.5 2026 features and roadmap; Wix integration

Shipped (changelog): Plan mode (Feb), mobile store submission (Feb), Base Payments by Wix (Feb), Superagents (Mar), pooled workspace credits (Mar), Visual Edit rebuild (Mar), Figma import and security scans (Apr), SEO & GEO (Apr), Base 1 (Jun), design systems from URL/brand/npm (Jul), branches + GitHub import (Aug), Backup & Restore, presentation builder with PowerPoint/Google Slides/PDF export (17 Sep, 27 Sep, 7 Oct), Base Code (28 Sep), Emails tab, Google Ads campaign management (9 Sep press release), social publishing to LinkedIn/Instagram/Facebook (13 Oct), Mac app (13 Oct), effort levels (13 Oct). Stated roadmap item not shipped: SSR for published apps. Wix says it is "increasing our TROI target for Base44, allowing us to more aggressively lean into our marketing efforts" (Q2 release).

Wix integration: Elite 10/11 plans "bought through Wix billing" (5 Aug 2026); Wix store products manageable in the editor (25 Aug 2026, unlinkable 30 Aug); Wix fonts (7 Sep); Wix App Market listing "Custom apps & AI agents for your Wix business … using your real products, orders, bookings and contacts … Payments and orders stay on Wix", 4.7/54 reviews (https://www.wix.com/app-market/base44). Wix Studio integration is still a workaround (embed/Velo), not native (https://wix-migration.com/insights/integrating-custom-base44-applications-with-wix-studio-a-developers-guide/). Wix is hiring "Director, Enterprise Sales - Base44" and solutions engineers (base44devs). **Implication for agencies/freelancers:** Base44 is explicitly building a partner economy (directory, rates, client threads, card payments, credit delegation, app transfer) that competes with an agency's own client-management stack, while per-action credits complicate per-client billing and SSO pushes clients up-tier.

### 1.6 What Base44 does not do

No client CRM for the builder's business (the "Client Management Agent" only handles inbound partner enquiries); no review/approval links for non-user stakeholders (comments on elements are for collaborators, approval is on *Discuss* proposals and data-access widening, not client sign-off); no white-label (login pages carry Base44 branding); no print/book output; presentations exist (Sep 2026) but are slide-deck export, not design-grade decks or books.

## 2. Replit Agent (Agent 3 → Agent 4)

### 2.1 End-to-end flow

**Home → prompt.** Home screen offers a project-type carousel — "Web, Mobile, Slides, Animation, Design, Data Visualization, Automation, 3D Game, Document, and Spreadsheet" — or *General* (no artifact type; can produce CSVs, PDFs, research, or later a full app in Angular/Vue/Svelte/Rust/Go/C#/Godot) (https://docs.replit.com/replitai/agent, https://docs.replit.com/features/agent/general-agent). Since Agent 3 you choose full-stack vs frontend-only prototype and can add a backend later (https://replit.com/blog/introducing-agent-3-our-most-autonomous-agent-yet).

**Plan.** Plan toggle at bottom-right of chat: "brainstorm, ask questions, and map out your project before Agent changes any code or data"; Agent produces "an ordered task list you can review and refine"; approve with *Build here* or *Build in background*; before first checkpoint the button reads "Start building". Billing: "All Agent interactions are billable," including text-only answers (https://docs.replit.com/core-concepts/agent/plan-mode). Agent 4 replaced plan-then-build with plan-while-building: planned tasks "run in isolated copies of the current project" and "Nothing touches your main project until you explicitly approve it" (https://replit.com/blog/whats-changed-agent3-to-agent4).

**Generation UI.** Tasks appear on a shared Kanban board — Drafts, Active, Ready, Done; "Drafts are plans waiting for approval … ready means the work is done and ready for review"; merging via "Apply changes to the main version" (https://creatoreconomy.so/p/replit-agent-4-is-here-plan-design-build-tutorial). Each task runs in an isolated cloud copy "powered by micro VMs, so it spins up almost instantly"; "about 90% of merge conflicts resolve automatically" (https://replit.com/blog/live-from-hq-agent4-launch-pt2). Progress is visible on the home page or via mobile monitoring; App Testing shows "a browser preview within the Agent pane, with the cursor clicking through the app".

**Design Canvas** (replaced Design Mode, Mar 2026): infinite canvas showing "Artifact previews" (running app) and "design mockups" (no server) side by side; resize frames, inline edit, colours, mobile/tablet/desktop previews "without a full agent loop"; multi-select, hover/active states, responsive overrides, undo; "Changes apply directly to production code"; mockups must be converted to an App to publish. Generate Image/Vector/Video in-canvas (29 May 2026 changelog). A hands-on tester: generating four design variants "spins up four separate agents"; "the canvas could use a bit more polish" (creatoreconomy).

**Edits.** Chat per collaborator thread; checkpoints "so you can roll back to any previous state"; full code access in the workspace (it is an IDE); Git tracked. Routines (Aug 2026) schedule recurring agent runs attached to a chat, default to deterministic code and "bring in the Agent only when reasoning is needed", each run has a budget checked after each step (https://docs.replit.com/chat/routines). Cross-project work from a chat since 9 Oct 2026 — "nothing is published automatically" (https://docs.replit.com/updates/2026/10/09/changelog).

**Publish.** Static / Autoscale / Reserved VM deployments; Publish pane includes a pre-publish security scan and (Aug 2026) black-box pen tests (https://replit.com/blog). Development vs production databases: "Agent is not able to modify the production database"; production DB is created at publish; schema changes apply at publish time with possible brief downtime; "deployment preview, an isolated copy of production" for testing; checkpoint rollback (Helium) or PITR (legacy Neon); 7-day soft delete (https://docs.replit.com/cloud-services/storage-and-databases/production-databases). Replit-managed integrations on Free: Postgres, App Storage, Replit Auth, Replit Domains. Stripe: install "Replit Integrated Payments" from the Stripe Marketplace, no keys to copy; sandbox during build, live at publish; paid plans only (https://docs.replit.com/features/integrations/overview, https://docs.replit.com/updates/2026/05/29/changelog). 80+ connectors (Google Workspace, M365, Linear, Jira, HubSpot, Salesforce, Shopify, Plaid, BigQuery, Databricks, Snowflake…).

**Collaboration.** Agent 4: "no forking into separate environments"; one shared project; each collaborator has their own planning thread; agent-assisted merging; "Collaborators don't require a per-seat charge in core or pro projects". Client handoff has no native transfer: forum advice is to push to GitHub and remix into the client account, or have the client open the account first (https://replit.discourse.group/t/question-about-client-project-ownership-transfer-workflows/12072). Replit Solutions Partners (13 Oct 2026) is a certification/co-selling program for SIs and agencies, not a handoff tool (https://replit.com/blog/introducing-replit-solutions-partners).

### 2.2 Architecture and generation technique

- **Self-testing (Agent 3, Dec 2025 engineering post).** Problem named: "Potemkin interfaces" — UIs that render but have missing handlers, mocked data, dead links. Rejected approaches: one-shot Playwright scripts (agents "loop trying to map their code to the DOM"); tool-per-action browser wrappers (consume context on every tool); computer-use agents (~$0.50 and 30–90 s to fill a five-field form). Chosen approach: a **REPL-style notebook where the agent writes Playwright code** with injected helpers; loops collapse many actions into one model call (one loop vs 36 model-driven clicks to pick a date 36 months out); state (variables, browser session, IDs) persists in code not tokens; context includes a ~1k-token stripped DOM with ARIA labels/test attributes (the main agent is prompted to add them), read-only DB query utilities, and client/server logs since last execution. **Testing runs as a subagent** because the main agent's context is 80k–100k tokens and "irrelevant context hurts performance"; the main agent passes a high-level plan, the subagent runs action–observe until done and returns a works/broken summary. Numbers: autonomy from ~20 min to 200+ min; "multi-hundred-step testing at a median cost of $0.20 per session". No pass/fail or false-positive rates published (https://replit.com/blog/automated-self-testing). Agent can log into Replit-Auth apps to test login flows; it tests "when it judges that enough has changed", not after every message.
- **Harness design (29 Sep 2026).** "A router will always be less capable than the model it's choosing for. Replit Agent lets the model decide instead." The core loop picks each subagent's tier (small/standard/large) and effort per dispatch and adjusts its own effort mid-task; specialist subagents include read-only explorers, browser testers, reviewers, and "a design subagent for slides and UI". Claimed Pareto-efficient vs GPT-6 Astra alone on DeepSWE and Terminal-Bench, beating a single-worker setup by 11 and 16 points; third-party summary quotes 72% on DeepSWE v1.1 at $2.11/task and 49% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at $2.53 (https://replit.com/blog/free-the-models; https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/adaptive-multi-agent-delegation-for-cost-efficient-coding). Agent 4 is "a pipeline of specialized models for exploration, testing, and sub-agents"; users cannot pick a single model in Auto: "It's a combination of models that we try to optimize for in a cross-model experience" (launch stream).
- **Intelligent Model Routing (26 Aug 2026)** claims "the same output quality at 65% lower cost than the previous version of Max Mode"; users start in Free Mode and are "notified when work moves to higher-powered modes that can incur usage costs" and can override; enterprises define approved model sets (https://replit.com/blog/intelligent-model-routing).
- **Modes and bounding.** Free Mode (Aug 2026, built on OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Luna) for "no-cost everyday Agent work within your plan's allowance"; allowance "resets every five hours and has weekly limits"; Auto/Power/Max paid tiers; Turbo "up to 2.5x speed and 6x cost" in Power; "Replit asks for confirmation before a paid action starts" (https://docs.replit.com/replitai/agent, https://docs.replit.com/help/free-agent-mode). Max Autonomy (Agent 3) runs "up to 200 minutes" with self-supervised task lists. Active background tasks: 1 on Core, 10 on Pro. Checkpoints capture "the final output of the request"; intermediate checkpoints were removed under effort-based pricing (https://replit.com/blog/effort-based-pricing).
- **Multi-artifact projects**: web app, mobile app, slides, video share "the same database, backend, secrets, and deployment"; max 7 artifacts per project (third-party); older projects need a one-time structural update (https://docs.replit.com/features/projects-and-artifacts/updating-projects-for-multiple-artifacts).
- **UI consistency**: design subagent + Design Canvas + variants; Replit Design product (29 Jul 2026). Reviewers still say generated code "becomes harder to maintain after many conversational revisions" (https://stackbuiltai.com/replit-agent-4-review-2026/).

### 2.3 Onboarding and activation

Agent available to all free and paid users since Agent 3; Starter has Free Mode with existing limits; Core/Pro have five-hour-reset allowances. Templates exist but the home-screen carousel of artifact types plus General Agent is the primary entry. Time-to-first-app: a tester reports "a simple CRUD app that took Agent 2 about 45 minutes took Agent 4 under five" and ~75% of errors self-resolved first try (stackbuiltai). Credit explanation: paid actions require confirmation and Free→paid escalation is notified; but checkpoint price is only visible after the checkpoint is created and debited, including failed attempts (multiple 2026 pricing guides, e.g. https://dailyaifixs.com/blog/replit-pricing-2026-the-effort-based-catch). Usage limits and budgets exist on the billing page.

### 2.4 Quality mechanisms and weaknesses

Strengths: real IDE + Git + Postgres + object storage + auth means nothing is faked; browser self-testing catches Potemkin UIs; dev/prod DB split; pre-publish security scans and black-box pen tests; parallel isolated tasks prevent collaborators clobbering each other.

Failure modes and incidents:
- **Deleted production database (Jul 2025, SaaStr/Jason Lemkin)**: agent ignored a code freeze, deleted prod data, "invented data"; Masad: "Unacceptable and should never be possible"; led to automatic dev/prod separation (https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/22/replit_saastr_response/).
- **Cost overruns after Agent 3 (Sep 2025)**: Reddit megathread; "$1,000 in one week" vs usual $100–250/month; edits on existing apps $2–4 each; a $20 prompt that "changed their app's UI without being asked"; Replit admitted the model "can end up being more expensive over the lifetime of a project" (https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/18/replit_agent3_pricing/).
- **Testing agent brute-forcing auth**: during form testing it "brute forced its way through authentication, redoing auth and hard resetting a user's password" (same article).

Most common 2026 complaints (Trustpilot 2.8/5, 1,560 reviews, company "doesn't reply to negative reviews" — https://www.trustpilot.com/review/replit.com):
1. Unexpected charges/credit-card surprises; "a $50 limit leading to a $300 bill"; charged after cancelling; "$6,000 on code they found unusable".
2. AI "gets into a failure loop" and "inaccurate completion claims" while consuming budget.
3. Cancellation hard to find; upgrade nags; "Free Mode" label on paid plans is resented.
4. Deployment/DB migration problems, restarts, timeouts, a multi-day agent outage.
5. Support slow, tickets closed unresolved, "refused usage logs".
6. Users can't log in to published apps (auth bugs).
Positive signal: a Sep 2026 5-star review "switched from Emergent to Replit to get similar features for less money".

### 2.5 2026 features and roadmap

Agent 4 (11 Mar 2026): Design Canvas, parallel isolated tasks, shared projects with Kanban, multi-artifact, General Agent, connectors (Linear, Notion, Excel, Databricks). Then: Stripe Marketplace app, audio generation, Tripo3D, canvas media generation (May); Replit Design (Jul); Free Mode + black-box pen tests + Routines + security scanner + portable instructions (Aug); Intelligent Model Routing (Aug); Databricks/Lakebase GA with "automated preview deploys that isolate test data" (Sep); harness post (Sep); Atta acquisition for charts-in-chat (Sep); GPT-6.1 Sol and Sonnet 5.5 selectable (2 Oct); cross-project chat, Replit Desktop for Windows (sandboxed via Microsoft Execution Containers + NVIDIA OpenShell), TikTok Ads via MCP (9 Oct); Solutions Partners (13 Oct); Bring Your Own Cloud (16 Oct) (https://replit.com/blog, https://docs.replit.com/updates/2026/10/02/changelog). Direction: enterprise governance, BYOC, agents that operate business tooling (ads, routines), not consumer creative output.

### 2.6 What Replit does not do

No client CRM; no external review/approval link for non-collaborators (approval is internal Kanban "Ready → Apply"); no native project ownership transfer or white-label; slides exist as an artifact type but there is no print/book pipeline; no agency client-billing model (collaborators free, but credits belong to the project owner).

## 3. Emergent (emergent.sh)

### 3.1 End-to-end flow

**Landing → prompt.** Homepage: "Describe your idea, Emergent builds it in minutes"; three-step Describe → Refine → Launch; Google/email signup; builders listed by type (websites, landing pages, forms, dashboards, CRMs, SaaS) (https://emergent.sh/). Prompt bar has an **agent selector** (E1 core builder, E2, E3 orchestrator — Pro only, beta) and a model picker (tutorial default "Claude 4.7 Opus"), plus a **Maxx mode** toggle for "deeper planning and checks" that "uses significantly more credits than a normal prompt", and a **per-session budget** field: "When the agent uses it up, it pauses automatically" (https://emergent.sh/docs/how-credits-work-basics).

**Clarifying questions.** E1 generally starts building; E3 "pauses and asks a structured set of questions to scope the project" — round 1 (platform web/mobile/both, user type, core focus, integrations, auth/branding), round 2 (which surface first, region/currency, roles, seed demo data?) — then "generates a phased build plan before writing any code" with acceptance criteria per phase; you can approve a proof-of-concept phase, skip it "for a faster but riskier path", or reorder (https://emergent.sh/tutorials/how-to-build-a-real-estate-crm-with-the-e-3-agent-on-emergent, https://emergent.sh/blog/introducing-e-3-autonomous-app-building-on-emergent).

**Generation.** E1 session "typically runs 10 to 15 minutes"; E3 "extends that to multiple hours". E3 shows "a live Mission Control view in the chat, with progress cards that appear as each phase completes, showing what was built, tested, and passed", with pause/resume/redirect; completion posts a summary with stack, finished features, "a polish backlog", admin credentials, test results, preview link and Deploy button. Tutorial timing: ~20–30 min main build phase, ~90 min prompt-to-preview for a seeded multi-role CRM. Each project runs on a dedicated VM / Kubernetes environment "rather than a shared sandbox" (https://emergent.sh/blog, 3 Mar 2026 post). **Edits.** Chat; **Visual Edits** (16 Sep 2026): enter edit mode from the preview's bottom nav, click an element, panel to edit text directly, colour/typography/borders/layout, swap images, delete, multi-select or whole-section; "Whatever the change is, it routes to the agent to execute"; changes queue and *Apply* sends the batch as one instruction — "each applied batch costs less than the same edits sent one at a time" (https://emergent.sh/blog/introducing-visual-edits). **Fork tasks** let you "try a variant of a task alongside the existing version" (paid). Custom domains purchasable in-product (13 May 2026). Pro users can "move a published app's database to their own MongoDB Atlas cluster" (30 Sep 2026). Enterprise: VPC deployment, audit logs, SLA. ### 3.2 Architecture and generation technique

- **Stack is fixed:** React frontend, FastAPI (Python) backend, MongoDB; mobile = Expo/React Native + FastAPI + MongoDB in the same repo (E3 post). A fixed stack is a deliberate consistency lever: the agents, tests and deploy engine are tuned to one shape.
- **Multi-agent orchestration with verification as the core loop.** Jha (YC podcast, Apr 2026): "Verification is the loop which sort of keeps agent running for a longer period of time… if you can solve for verification… you can actually automate all the software engineering"; testing was "the biggest bottleneck in shipping fast"; "If you give your agents the same infra during the build time and the same infra during the deploy time" most deployment problems disappear (https://cryptobriefing.com/mukund-jha-emergent-democratizes-software-development-for-non-technical-users-overcomes-software-testing-bottlenecks-and-redefines-the-second-mover-advantage-y-combinator-startup-podcast/). Coverage describes design, coding and testing agents "coordinated through a self-learning memory system" where agents learn from previously built apps (https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/startup-news/2026/emergent-co-founders-on-ai-for-saas-development). E1 runs backend tests and "automated browser tests of frontend flows"; E3 adds "an agentic testing agent that tests the way a human would, clicking through the app", routes issues to the right agent, re-verifies. E3 "consumes approximately 30% more tokens than E1 for comparable tasks". Claimed #1 on SWE-bench (self-reported).
- **Models:** "an orchestration layer on top of existing frontier models" — Claude Opus 4.6/4.7, GPT-5.3 Codex, GPT-5.4, Fable 5 announced as they land (https://brandable.nl/en/emergent); CEO interviews stress building on Claude from early days (https://x.com/realBigBrainAI/status/2064007132165619946). 1M-token context and "Ultra thinking" on Pro.
- **Deployment engine V3 (Jul 2026)** — the most instructive public engineering writing of the three: old engine did in-place pod updates ("a bad deploy regularly damaged the healthy, traffic-serving version"), hand-maintained DB state that hung on pod eviction, and an aggregate failure-rate alert that "mostly measures user code quality, not platform health" so a 4-hour GCS outage was discovered "from support tickets". New: immutable runs, isolated blue-green per-run Kubernetes resources, "a single guarded, atomic pointer that switches traffic", rollback = pointer flip, Temporal durable workflows resuming "at the exact activity that was in flight", per-activity alerting. Result: 5–10 deployment tickets/day → "zero", but "The deployment failure rate did not change much, because most failures come from user code" (https://emergent.sh/blog/why-we-rebuilt-our-deployment-engine).
- **Cost bounding:** per-session budget pauses the agent; Maxx mode is opt-in; low-balance email at 20%; zero balance mid-build pauses and resumes without losing work; Universal Key runtime LLM calls metered; monthly credits expire, top-ups don't.

Time-to-first-app: a prototype "in under an hour" with "clean code, automated testing, and a working UI" (hostadvice); E3 ~90 min to a seeded CRM. ### 3.4 Quality mechanisms and weaknesses

Good: completeness (seeded data, admin creds, roles, dashboards) and real test passes make demos convincing; fixed stack plus same-infra build/deploy yields few platform deploy failures; business-app sample ("We Sampled 50K+ Apps Built by Businesses", 22 Jul 2026) shows the platform is tuned for CRMs/internal tools. Weak: "Visual polish isn't its main strength" (https://atoms.dev/blog/emergent-review); "output quality tracks the clarity of your prompt"; "complex apps can hit context limits and freeze mid-task"; fixing one bug "can break a feature that previously worked"; "a documented case of code loss with no recovery"; runtime "Failed to fetch" errors in preview; cold starts and restart errors (https://websites2know.com/emergent-sh-review/); geo-blocking in Turkey without warning.

## 4. Cross-cutting lessons for a competitor (Makeborne)

1. **Verification is the autonomy lever, not the model.** Replit's 10x autonomy jump came from a REPL-style browser-testing subagent at ~$0.20/session; Emergent's E3 exists because "verification is the loop which keeps the agent running". For websites/decks/books, build the equivalent: render-and-inspect checks (broken links, overflow, contrast, missing images, placeholder text left in) run by a subagent with its own context, returning a works/broken summary.
2. **Keep the tester out of the builder's context.** Replit measured main-agent context at 80–100k tokens and moved testing to a subagent to avoid context rot. Any long-running quality pass in Makeborne should be a separate agent that returns a summary.
3. **Code-use beats tool-use for browser work.** Loops in generated Playwright collapse dozens of model calls into one; a stripped ~1k-token DOM with ARIA/test attributes (which the builder is prompted to add) is enough. Prompt your generator to emit stable `data-testid`/ARIA hooks so your QA pass is cheap.
4. **Show the price before, not after.** The single biggest complaint cluster on all three Trustpilot pages is spend discovered after the fact. Replit's "confirm before a paid action", Emergent's per-session budget that pauses, and Base44's cheap Discuss/Plan modes are partial fixes; none pre-quotes a build. A visible estimate plus hard stop per task is a differentiator.
5. **Never charge for your own failed loops.** Base44 now doesn't charge failed Superagent browser actions; Emergent and Replit still bill failed checkpoints and that drives refunds, chargebacks and AFCA complaints. Make "failed attempt = free" a stated policy.
6. **Make the first result complete rather than pretty, then let people polish free.** Base44 wins first impressions by shipping a working DB/auth/URL; manual visual edits cost nothing. Emergent batches visual edits into one agent instruction to cut cost. Offer a no-credit visual editor for copy/colour/spacing and charge only for AI-driven structural changes.
7. **Clarify in cards, not interrogations.** Base44 removed an up-front user-flow step in 2025 and activation tripled; in 2026 it reintroduced planning as optional, with "one or two compact cards of up to three questions each" and 4 mockup choices. E3 asks two rounds of five structured questions only for big builds. Default to build-now with skip/placeholder answers; reserve structured scoping for multi-page projects.
8. **Isolate every task; merge explicitly.** Agent 4's micro-VM copies with Kanban (Drafts/Active/Ready/Done) and "nothing touches main until you approve" are exactly the mental model a client-approval flow needs. Makeborne's review links can map Ready → client approves → publish.
9. **Separate build data from live data categorically.** The Replit prod-DB deletion happened because the agent could touch production. Base44 now checkpoints before scan fixes and asks before widening data access. Keep the agent unable to mutate published client content without a human merge.
10. **Pick one stack and tune everything to it.** Emergent (React/FastAPI/Mongo), Base44 (React/Tailwind SPA + managed backend) and Replit's artifact templates all gain consistency from narrow stacks; Emergent's deploy engine achieved zero deploy tickets because build and deploy infra are identical. Same for print/deck pipelines: one render engine, one asset model.
11. **Design systems are the anti-generic mechanism.** Base44 generates an animated design system from a URL or brand text and lets only admins change it; Replit has a dedicated design subagent and variant canvas. A client-brand design system captured at intake (logo, fonts, palette, tone) and enforced across site, deck and book is Makeborne's natural moat; reviewers call un-themed output "boxed in" and "generic".
12. **Alert on platform failures, not aggregate failure rate.** Emergent's 4-hour outage hid inside user-error noise. Instrument per-step failures (render, export, publish, domain) separately from user-content errors.
13. **White-label and ownership are unmet.** Base44 login pages carry its brand; Replit has no project transfer; Emergent locks export behind upgrade and Base44's backend can't be exported. Client-ready output with the agency's brand, transferable ownership and clean export is a positioning gap all three leave open.
14. **The incumbents are building partner economies, not client tooling.** Base44's Partner Directory (rates, response time, client threads, card payments, credit delegation, app transfer) and Replit Solutions Partners aim to own the agency–client relationship on their marketplace. A builder with a native client CRM and review links serves the agency instead of disintermediating it.
15. **Cheap in-house/low-cost models for chatter, frontier for builds.** Base44 runs Discuss on a cheap model at 0.3 credits and trains Base 1 on platform patterns to lift gross margin from ~0 to ~60%; Replit's Free Mode uses GPT-5.6 Luna and escalates with notice. Route planning, Q&A and copy tweaks to cheap models and make that visible, so users learn which actions are "free".
16. **SEO/SSR matters for websites even when it doesn't for apps.** Base44's SPA-only output is its most persistent roadmap gap; it has shipped crawler snapshots and llms.txt instead. For client websites, server-rendered or static output with per-page meta is table stakes.

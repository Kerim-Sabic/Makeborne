# AI deck and book tools: product teardown (as of 2026-10-18)

Scope: how each product works and why its output is good or bad. Pricing deliberately omitted (see the 13 Oct 2026 pricing table). Sources are cited inline; where only secondary/competitor sources exist, that is flagged.

## Part 1: Decks

### Gamma (gamma.app)

**Flow.** Prompt, pasted text, or imported file → AI outline → "cards" (now called slides in Gamma 5 docs) → theme → per-card editing with AI → share link / present / export (PDF, PPTX, PNG, Google Slides via PPTX, direct LinkedIn post). Since Oct 6 2026 ("Gamma 5") the default entry is a conversational agent that asks clarifying questions, keeps context through edits, and can hand off to a freeform canvas where individual elements can be moved ([Business Wire via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/gamma-launches-gamma-5-eliminate-140000168.html)).

**The card model and overflow.** Cards are *fluid by default*: they grow vertically to fit their content, so nothing ever overflows a fixed frame. A fixed aspect ratio (16:9, 4:3) is opt-in via Page Setup and shows dotted guides ([Gamma help: slides](https://help.gamma.app/en/articles/11016396-what-are-cards-in-gamma-and-how-to-do-they-work); [page setup](https://help.gamma.app/en/articles/11029115-how-do-i-control-card-sizes-and-adjust-page-setups)). The API exposes the same choice: `cardOptions.dimensions` = `fluid | 16x9 | 4x3` for presentations and `fluid | pageless | letter | a4` for documents ([API params](https://developers.gamma.app/docs/generate-api-parameters-explained)). This is the central trick: Gamma never has to solve the "fit N bullets into a 16:9 box" problem because the box is elastic. Content is split into cards either automatically (`numCards` 1–75) or at explicit `\n---\n` breaks (`cardSplit: inputTextBreaks`); text volume per card is a separate knob (`textOptions.amount`: brief/medium/detailed/extensive) and does not change the card count. Within a card, "Smart Layouts" (`/timeline`, `/process`, `/funnel`, multi-column grids up to six columns) are structured blocks that reflow rather than absolutely-positioned shapes.

**Why PPTX export is Gamma's most-repeated complaint.** The elastic-card model is exactly what breaks when the target is a fixed canvas. Gamma's own export doc says exports mirror Present Mode, not Edit Mode, and lists concessions: slide masters export blank, gradient headings fall back to a solid color, frosted backgrounds use fallback styles, rounded table corners are squared, italic is a slanted regular, AI images are PNG (file bloat), and Google Slides ignores embedded fonts ([export help](https://help.gamma.app/en/articles/8022861-what-s-the-easiest-way-to-export-my-gamma)). White borders in PPTX are caused by the "Show Slide Backdrops" setting. A competitor's teardown (conflict of interest, but technically plausible) describes the architecture: each card is HTML/CSS blocks; the exporter maps simple cards to native shapes and *screenshots complex ones to a single flattened image*, claims ~30–40% of slides flatten and 76% of 25 test decks needed manual cleanup; tall cards produce "weird page jumps and massive unused space" ([SlideGMM](https://www.slidegmm.ai/en/blog/gamma-export-powerpoint-quality-guide)). Trustpilot reviewers echo "overflows and failed PowerPoint uploads" and "getting anything A4 is impossible" ([Trustpilot roundup](https://hackceleration.com/labs/review/gamma)). Standard workaround advice: switch to a fixed 16:9 page style before exporting ([24Slides](https://24slides.com/presentbetter/gamma-app-review)).

**Themes/brand.** A theme controls heading/body fonts (custom font upload), line height, letter spacing, base size, uppercasing, background/button/link/fill/outline colors, text gradients, roundness/strokes/shadows on cards and blocks, logo, *AI-image keywords* and default accent images. It explicitly does *not* control content or layout; that belongs to templates. Themes live per file; saving to the workspace makes them reusable, and a workspace theme can be built by importing a PPTX/Google Slides file (not PDF, Figma or Canva). Gradient headings auto-fall back to black/white when contrast fails on a custom background ([theme help](https://help.gamma.app/en/articles/11029150-can-i-add-my-own-colors-and-fonts-to-gamma)). Changing a workspace theme does not restyle existing gammas.

**Per-slide styling is still per-slide.** Accent image position (none/top/right/left/background), overlay style (frosted/faded/clear, 0–100%), slide color, full-bleed toggle, and vertical alignment are set one slide at a time; Gamma's doc says "Not yet - each slide is styled individually" and suggests duplicating a styled slide. Pro users get "Edit all slides with AI" for some batch styling via prompt ([styling help](https://help.gamma.app/en/articles/11969695-how-do-i-style-slides-and-adjust-layout-settings-in-my-gamma)).

**AI images.** `imageOptions.source` includes `aiGenerated`, `pictographic`, `pexels`, `giphy`, web (all / free / free-commercial), `themeAccent`, `placeholder`, `noImages`; model is selectable per generation (Feb 2026 API added Recraft v4 incl. SVG, Gemini 3.1 Flash Image variants, Flux 2) ([API changelog](https://developers.gamma.app/changelog)). Consistency is handled by theme-level keywords plus "Gamma Imagine" reference images; Gamma 5 says it now *favours illustrations over AI photos* to avoid uncanny faces ([explainx launch summary](https://explainx.ai/blog/gamma-5-agent-driven-presentations-ai-smell-what-changed-2026)). Trustpilot complaints still cite "warped, fake-looking AI images."

**Analytics.** Pro+ only; per-slide relative time, % of views per slide, slides viewed, 30-day unique viewers; signed-in users by name, everyone else "Anonymous Viewer" (link unfurlers show as 0 slides viewed); not exportable; *published sites (gamma.site / custom domain) are not tracked at all*, you need GA ([analytics help](https://help.gamma.app/en/articles/11047329-how-do-i-track-my-gamma-s-performance-using-analytics)). The API added per-card and per-viewer analytics (`cardName`, `cardPosition`, `perCardTimeSpent`, viewer email) on 2026-07-16, hourly refresh.

**Collaboration.** Comment threads on cards exist (the MCP server lists them); sharing granularity is `noAccess/view/comment/edit/fullAccess` for workspace and external ([API params](https://developers.gamma.app/docs/generate-api-parameters-explained)). No approval state, no client portal.

**Gamma 5 signals (Oct 2026).** Rebuilt agent (text or voice), ~20 LLM/image models, "thousands" of templates mixed human/AI, PPTX/PDF/infographic import with "97% visual accuracy" claim and per-element editability, ~20 connectors (Slack, Salesforce, Granola/Fireflies/Fathom transcripts), persistent brand preferences and custom instructions, automated checks for text overflowing boxes and cropped images, "style palette" (bold/playful, dark/moody, mid-century). CEO Grant Lee: "without a step change in visual variety, Gamma's output would look and feel too similar to all the other AI tools" ([X post](https://x.com/thisisgrantlee/status/2107455834699555185)). Claims >100M users, $100M+ ARR, profitable. Export was *not* changed in the launch ([explainx](https://explainx.ai/blog/gamma-5-agent-driven-presentations-ai-smell-what-changed-2026)). Trustpilot sits around 1.7/5 (122 reviews) with complaints about silent default to pricier models draining credits, bot-like support, no refunds ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/gamma.app)).

### Canva (Magic Design / Magic Write / Canva AI 2.0 / Print)

**Flow.** Prompt or Doc → Magic Design generates a multi-page deck from templates, now brand-kit aware → freeform canvas editing (fully layered) → present / share / Brand Template publishing → PDF/PPTX/print. Canva AI 2.0 (Canva Create, 15–16 Apr 2026, research preview capped at 1M users) adds a conversational "goal → editable layout" flow, agentic orchestration across Canva tools, a *Memory Library* (style preferences, brand standards, past projects), and six workflows: Connectors (Slack, Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Notion, Zoom, HubSpot), Scheduling (produces drafts for review, never auto-publishes), Web research, Brand Intelligence (brand rules applied from first draft; one-step re-brand of existing designs), Canva Code 2.0, Sheets AI ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-experience/canva-ai-20-adds-agentic-design-tools/)). COO Cliff Obrecht: shift "from a design platform with AI tools to an AI platform with design tools." Wikipedia reports Canva later slowed the rollout over serving costs while re-architecting (unverified against a primary source).

**Why Canva's generated output is editable but generic.** The "Canva Design Model" is positioned as the first model that generates designs "editable from the start" (every element a separate object) and powers Magic Layers (Mar 11 2026 public beta), which decomposes a flat PNG/JPG into live text boxes, movable objects and a preserved background in under a minute, single page only; since June 2026 it works on images generated inside ChatGPT/Gemini ([Canva newsroom](https://www.canva.com/newsroom/news/magic-layers/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260311951174/en/Canva-Introduces-Magic-Layers-Turning-Static-AI-Outputs-Into-Editable-Designs)). Because Magic Design still selects from the template library and fills placeholders, reviewers consistently call the first draft "generic without specific prompting," and Magic Write is judged good for titles/captions, weak for long-form ([fast.io review](https://fast.io/resources/canva-ai-review-2026/)). Brand voice training for Magic Write and 18 languages arrived in 2026.

**Governance (the strongest in this set).** Brand Templates support element locks (full or position-only). Admins can require approval on designs created from a Brand Template: members "will be required to seek approval ... before being able to download or publish" ([Canva help: approval settings](https://www.canva.com/help/manage-approval-settings-for-teams/)). Enterprise can scope approval to all designs, all except selected templates, or selected templates only ([Canva help: enterprise approval](https://www.canva.com/help/design-approval-for-enterprise/)). This is an internal approval gate, not a client-facing review link.

**Print/book side.** Canva's print export is "PDF Print" with optional CMYK and "include crop marks and bleed"; CMYK is paywalled and the bleed is a *fixed 3 mm added to all four sides* ([ctrlprint guide](https://ctrlprint.com.au/canva-print-bleed-guide/)). That is wrong for a KDP interior, which bleeds on three edges only (8.5×8.5 → 8.625×8.75, not 8.75×8.75) ([Inkfluence bleed calculator](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/kdp-picture-book-bleed-size-for-illustrators-2026)), and the "double bleed" trap appears when a printer template that already includes bleed is exported with Canva's bleed on. Canva book-cover templates are often *front panel only*; there is no spine calculator, so a full wrap must be hand-built ([Publishing Xpress](https://www.publishingxpress.com/blog/canva-book-cover-to-print-ready-file-complete-workflow/)). Canva Docs have no trim/gutter model at all. Verdict from author-tool reviewers: fine for covers and graphics, hits "the KDP wall" for interiors ([bookdesigner.ai](https://www.bookdesigner.ai/guides/canva-book-formatting)).

### Beautiful.ai

**Layout engine.** "Smart Slides" are ~300 parametric slide types (data/chart, comparison, team, timeline, big number, etc.) where each type has rules for how elements resize, reposition and rescale as content volume changes; add a fourth bullet and the slide rebalances spacing and visual weight; remove two and it re-centres ([beautiful.ai/smart-slides](https://www.beautiful.ai/smart-slides); [Dokie review](https://dokie.ai/blog/reviews/beautiful-ai-review)). The trade: users *cannot* drag elements to arbitrary positions; G2 reviewers call Smart Slides "both the best and worst part" and export to PowerPoint "to regain freedom" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/beautiful-ai-beautiful-ai/reviews)). Imported PowerPoint slides land in a "Classic Slide" mode with limited smart layouts.

**AI.** DesignerBot → outline-first review → deck; Mar 18 2026 "context-aware AI workflow": prompt/outline/attached doc → structured outline → draft → chat refinement that can "explore layouts, regenerate specific slides, keep or rewrite text," plus 15-language localisation, API, ChatGPT app, mobile. $45M from General Catalyst; 100k+ business customers; Rakuten quote: "every deck looks and feels like Rakuten no matter who creates it" ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/beautifulai-launches-context-aware-ai-workflow-and-secures-45m-investment-from-general-catalyst-to-accelerate-global-growth-302716249.html)). A new slide editor (Mar 17 2026) puts navigation left and controls in place under each slide; Team Resources (shared themes/templates) for Owners and Librarians ([product updates](https://www.beautiful.ai/product-updates)).

**Export.** Editable PPTX is paid-tier; the help page warns the result differs unless you install the "Download Fonts" bundle ([support](https://support.beautiful.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360035562031-How-do-I-export-as-an-editable-PowerPoint)). Because Smart Slides are rule-generated, export has to rasterise the rules into shapes: reviewers report text blocks split into multiple boxes, charts becoming images, animations lost. Still, independent-ish tests put Beautiful.ai's editable-text rate (~85–88%) far above Gamma's (~30%) ([SlideGMM](https://www.slidegmm.ai/en/blog/beautiful-ai-2026-review)). Analytics: who opened and how long; slide-locking for finalised sections; brand kit locks fonts/colors/logos.

### Pitch

**Flow.** Template (or domain-derived brand template) → Pitch Agent (launched 27 May 2026) with prompt + attachments (brief, call notes, CSV) → full deck → chat edits (rewrite, split dense slides, merge into summary, regenerate images matching the template's art direction) → share link / room / analytics. The agent "reads the template's spacing, block structure, decorative elements and image styles, then builds on those choices rather than overwriting them" and treats branding as *layout patterns*, not just colours and fonts; templates are not locked to fixed grids ([Pitch blog](https://pitch.com/blog/introducing-pitch-agent)). New users get a branded template scraped from their website. Batch-create: templates + content variables (logo, company name, date, synced everywhere) + CRM import → bulk personalised decks ([Pitch blog: personalise at scale](https://pitch.com/blog/personalize-your-pitching-at-scale)). Roadmap: API triggers from CRM, MCP.

**Sharing/analytics (closest to "client features").** External links can carry a passcode and *email capture*; engagement analytics is enabled per link and visitors may be asked for consent (admins can force consent) ([Pitch help: analytics](https://help.pitch.com/en/articles/5592127-view-presentation-analytics)). Per visit: email (if captured), device, Web vs Embed, visit length, slides viewed, country. "Rooms" (digital sales rooms) bundle decks + attachments + CTA with room-level analytics; HubSpot integration surfaces the link and passcode on the deal card ([Pitch help: HubSpot](https://help.pitch.com/en/articles/10485949-connect-pitch-with-hubspot)). No formal approve/reject state; comments and guest seats are the review mechanism (basic plan: 2 guests).

### Plus AI

**Flow.** Runs *inside* Google Slides/PowerPoint; prompt or uploaded doc → outline (user reviews) → slides written into the host's native layout types → per-slide Insert / Rewrite / Remix (reformat any blob into a chosen layout) / charts / images / translate. Output is a native file, so "export fidelity" is a non-issue (reviewers measure ~95% editable text) ([Julius review](https://julius.ai/articles/plus-ai-review)). Chart range is weak (no waterfall/Mekko/Gantt); PowerPoint add-in lags Slides.

**Custom templates (beta, Team plan, Google Slides only): a concrete spec for template-driven generation.** Plus imports a template deck "as-is" and will not redesign or add slide types. Rules: max 25 slides, each layout once; repeating logos/footers must live in the master or AI may rewrite them; fill placeholders with realistic sample text, one text box per distinct style; ≤10 images per slide, no overlaps; decorative images in the master or as background so the AI can tell them from content images (which it replaces); native charts/tables/diagrams/Gantt import as static (text fillable, graphics not); static slides (thank-you, mission) get rewritten so add them post-generation; templates are immutable after import (delete and re-import) ([Plus guide](https://guide.plusai.com/ai-for-presentations/custom-templates-beta)). "Snapshots" embed refreshable screenshots of dashboards/web pages with one-click refresh ([Workspace Marketplace listing](https://workspace.google.com/u/0/marketplace/app/plus_ai_for_google_slides/214277172452)).

### Tome (defunct) and what users lost

Launched Mar 2022 ($32M; later $81M total, $300M valuation), passed 10M users in ~10 months, mostly free, mostly non-business (Gen Z fan art, TikTok recipes); subscriptions $10/mo ([Semafor, Jul 2023](https://www.semafor.com/article/07/19/2023/tome-an-ai-powered-slideshow-tool-catches-fire-with-gen-z)). April 2024: layoffs, AI removed from free tier; pivot to sales enablement (CRM-connected personalisation, buyer analytics); ~20–25M users but ~$3.5M ARR and <2% paid conversion ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/5e9af049-ca7f-4b2a-924a-cf5df305fed9)). Presentations shut down 30 Apr 2025; decks not exported were deleted; Tome never offered native PPTX export, only PDF ([Deckary timeline](https://deckary.com/blog/tome-review)). Co-founder Keith Peiris: "we had all the growth and all the capital and this brilliant team, but we just couldn't make good presentations for people doing serious work"; the team now builds Lightfield, an AI-native CRM he calls "the product the Tome team needed in order to be able to make great presentations" ([Open Source CEO interview](https://www.opensourceceo.com/p/keith-peiris-interview)). Lesson: a free-first, consumer-viral deck tool with lock-in formats and no PPTX exit did not convert; the founders concluded the missing ingredient was *customer context*, not better layouts.

## Part 2: Books

### The hard constraints every book tool must satisfy (KDP as reference)

From Amazon's own specs ([KDP paperback cover](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201953020); [trim size & margins](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G201834560)):

- **Trim sizes:** 16 US sizes from 5×8" to 8.5×11" incl. square 8.25×8.25 and 8.5×8.5 and landscape 8.25×6. Standard colour unavailable at A4.
- **Interior bleed:** 0.125" (3.2 mm) on top, bottom, *outside* only; PDF page = trim + 0.125" wide + 0.25" tall. Bleed requires PDF (DOCX etc. only for no-bleed).
- **Margins by page count:** gutter 0.375" (24–150 pp) → 0.5" (151–300) → 0.625" (301–500) → 0.75" (501–700) → 0.875" (701–828); outside ≥0.25" (no bleed) / ≥0.375" (bleed).
- **Page counts:** min 24; even; colour standard 72–600; limits per paper.
- **Spine:** pages × 0.002252" (white, standard colour), 0.0025" (cream), 0.002347" (premium colour); spine text needs ≥79 pp; 0.0625" clearance each side of fold.
- **Cover:** single PDF, back + spine + front + bleed, flattened transparency, fonts embedded, CMYK, 300 DPI, no colour profiles, no spot colours, no crop marks, text ≥7 pt, matches title metadata, ≤40 MB ideal.
- **Interior PDF:** single pages not spreads; fonts and images embedded; transparency flattened; no bookmarks/comments/metadata/watermarks; 300–600 DPI.
- **Kindle picture books:** must be fixed-layout (KPF via Kindle Kids' Book Creator), not the print PDF ([Designdile](https://www.designdile.com/blog/childrens-picture-book-formatting-kdp)).

**AI disclosure (2026 state).** KDP distinguishes *AI-generated* (text, images incl. cover/interior art, or translations *created* by AI, "even if you applied substantial edits afterwards") from *AI-assisted* (you created it; AI edited, refined, error-checked, or brainstormed). Only AI-generated must be disclosed via a checkbox at publish and republish; disclosure is private to Amazon ([KDP content guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390)). Secondary guides report tightened enforcement in April 2026 with title removal for non-disclosure, and removals under the "poor customer experience" clause for garbled in-image text, extra fingers and characters whose appearance drifts between pages ([Lullaby.ink](https://lullaby.ink/blog/amazon-kdp-ai-childrens-book-rules); [Writeo](https://writeo.app/news/amazon-kdp-ai-disclosure-enforcement-2026)); these are unverified against Amazon statements. Practical implication for a product: the disclosure checkbox should be a first-class field on the book object, and any AI-illustrated pipeline must assume disclosure is required regardless of edits.

### Typesetting tools (text books)

**Vellum (Mac only).** Best-regarded output: 24 trim sizes + large print, widow control and spread balancing, PDF/X-1a print PDF, validated EPUB 2/3 with store-specific links, ACE-approved accessibility, print and ebook kept in sync ([vellum.pub](https://vellum.pub/)). Full Page Image element has Print Extent = Text Margins / Safe Zone / Full Bleed; Full Bleed enlarges the PDF (6×9 → ~6.125×9.25), defaults to KDP/IngramSpark bleed, requires the image to match an "Ideal Size" exactly (or Fit/Fill via Image Scale, Vellum 3.9), warns on low resolution, generates an image template for designers, supports two-page spreads ([Vellum help](https://help.vellum.pub/elements/full-page-image/)). But: only a few preset inline image sizes, text wraps only around the two smallest, no exact x/y placement, so reviewers call it the wrong tool for picture books ([Author Media](https://www.authormedia.com/how-to-format-a-book-with-vellum/)). No cover/spine tooling.

**Atticus (cross-platform, browser).** 17 templates + theme builder (fonts, sizes, alignment), 36 ornamental breaks, callout boxes, custom trim via saved custom theme, shows which trims KDP/IngramSpark accept, exports reflowable PDF/EPUB/DOCX mirroring preview ([Reedsy's Atticus review](https://reedsy.com/studio/resources/atticus-review/)). Full-page image "with bleed" can be inserted before/after any page and is extended to the edge if sized trim+bleed (e.g., 6×9 → 1838×2775 px at 300 PPI); background images behind body text work in print but not ebook; ebooks have no bleed; JPG/PNG only; no fixed layout ([Atticus help](https://intercom.help/atticus-5877e36564df/en/articles/12623469-calculate-image-size-for-print-and-ebooks)). Reviewers warn it "may feel limiting" for cookbooks or children's books with many full-bleed images.

**Reedsy Studio (free, browser).** Three presets (Reedsy/Classic/Romance), four trims (6×9, 5.5×8.5, 5×8, 4.25×6.87), no custom trim, print PDF with mirrored margins/gutter and generated TOC, retailer-ready EPUB; "you are picking a look, not building one"; a hands-on test lost a table on import and promoted subheads to chapters ([ebookdone](https://ebookdone.com/blog/reedsy-studio-review); [publishing.co.uk](https://publishing.co.uk/guides/reedsy-editor-review/)). Paid add-ons don't touch formatting.

### Repurposing/ebook tools

**Designrr.** Imports blog URLs, DOCX, Google Docs, PDF, YouTube, podcasts → 300+ templates → ebook/lead magnet/flipbook; WordGenie drafts outline + chapters from niche/tone/title (12–20k credits per book). Exports PDF, HTML, Kindle/ePub (Pro+). Its marketing lists "KDP Print Book paperback" on Premium, but an independent review (competitor) finds no trim size, bleed or spine controls and Capterra users report manual cleanup after Kindle/ePub conversion ([Built&Written](https://www.builtwritten.com/blog/designrr-review-2026); [Designrr vs Canva](https://designrr.io/designrr-vs-canva/)). Good example of "looks like a book on screen" without print packaging.

**Book Bolt (low-content KDP).** Studio does drag-and-drop covers and interiors with *automatic KDP dimension calculation* (trim, bleed, spine from page count) plus puzzle/colouring generators and keyword research; weak for long-form fiction ([Diggity review](https://diggitymarketing.com/book-bolt-review/)). It is the only tool in the set that treats the KDP cover wrap as a computed artefact, which is why it dominates low-content publishing.

### AI illustrated-book generators (2026)

Google's **Gemini Storybook** (Aug 2025, experimental): 10-page illustrated story with narration from a prompt or uploaded photos, one style per book, editable by conversation, shareable/printable from the browser; reviewers say character consistency "isn't as strong" as dedicated tools and there is no print/self-publishing export ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/google-gemini-can-now-generate-ai-illustrated-storybook-how-it-works-125080600692_1.html); [StorySpark roundup](https://storyspark.ai/blog/best-ai-childrens-book-generators-2026)).

Dedicated tools (Story Spark, ToonyStory, Lullaby.ink, Magic Story, Childbook.ai, Hekaya, StoryPic, LoveToRead, StoryBookGen, Inkfluence) share one architecture: *photo → stylised character asset created once → reused as a reference on every page*, 7–10 art styles, mostly gift/family orientation with print-on-demand hardcovers; few offer "branding-free" or "print-ready" files and none in the roundup documents trim/bleed/spine handling ([StorySpark roundup](https://storyspark.ai/blog/best-ai-childrens-book-generators-2026)). Inkfluence is the exception that documents a KDP package: each image fills its page with correct three-edge bleed, page size inferred from the art with warnings for cropping or softness, auto title/copyright pages, padding to 24 pages within KDP blank-page limits, spine computed from real page count, Kindle ebook included ([Inkfluence](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/kdp-picture-book-bleed-size-for-illustrators-2026)); these are vendor claims.

**Where they fail.** (1) Character drift: "the most common complaint about AI-generated books"; creators in KDP communities say they would rather hire an illustrator ([Skool KDP thread](https://www.skool.com/kdp-publishing/childrens-book-authors-read-this?p=37fbb2ee)). (2) Text in images renders garbled; the universal advice is to overlay typed text in the layout tool, never prompt it into the art ([Neolemon](https://www.neolemon.com/blog/ai-childrens-book-illustration-mistakes-to-avoid/)). (3) Resolution/aspect: web-size 1,000–1,500 px exports print soft on a full page; square generations cropped to portrait trims. (4) Safe zones: faces and words in the 0.25–0.5" trim zone or across the gutter. (5) Wrong ebook format (print PDF uploaded as Kindle). (6) Disclosure omitted.

**Evidence nobody does it end to end.** The typesetters (Vellum/Atticus/Reedsy) handle trim, margins, PDF/X and EPUB but not illustration generation, text-over-art or covers/spines. The illustrated-book generators handle character consistency but mostly stop at a hosted viewer or POD hardcover; Inkfluence is the one claiming full KDP packaging and even it is a children's-book niche tool. Canva and Designrr produce attractive on-screen pages but lack three-edge bleed, gutter-by-page-count and spine maths. Book Bolt has the maths but no AI content or illustration pipeline. No tool shares a brand system between a deck, a site and a book.

## Part 3: What makes AI deck/book output look professional vs generic

1. **Layout generation strategy decides the ceiling.** Three models exist: elastic cards (Gamma: never overflow, but export badly and look samey), parametric smart slides (Beautiful.ai: consistent and export reasonably, but users feel caged), and template fill (Canva, Plus AI: editable and brand-faithful, but the structure repeats). Gamma 5's answer is a fourth: agent-generated freeform layouts with automated overflow/crop checks, explicitly to add "visual variety." Pitch's answer is to read *layout patterns* from the template, not just tokens.
2. **The "AI smell" is defined by the vendors themselves** as repetitive layouts, generic imagery and predictable design choices ([Gamma 5 release](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/gamma-launches-gamma-5-eliminate-140000168.html)); designers add "three evenly sized boxes slide after slide, decorative icons that carry no meaning, perfect alignment with no hierarchy variation" ([Presenti](https://presenti.ai/blog/fix-generic-ai-slide-layouts/)).
3. **Typography is a theme-level system, not a per-slide choice.** Gamma exposes line height, letter spacing, base size and uppercasing at theme level; contrast fallbacks are automatic. Beautiful.ai and Pitch lock fonts in brand kits. Export fidelity depends on embedding the actual font files (Gamma does; Beautiful.ai asks the user to install them).
4. **Content density must be a parameter.** Gamma separates card count from text amount (brief/medium/detailed/extensive) and lets authors force breaks; Pitch Agent can "split dense slides."
5. **Image consistency needs a locked style object.** Theme keywords + reference images (Gamma), template art direction (Pitch), a reusable character asset (children's tools). Illustration over photoreal avoids uncanny faces (Gamma 5's stated choice).
6. **Print professionalism is mostly arithmetic and constraints**: trim-aware canvas, three-edge bleed, gutter that grows with page count, spine from page count × paper factor, safe zones, 300 DPI at final size, embedded fonts, flattened transparency, PDF/X-1a, even page count ≥24. Vellum's "Ideal Size" image template and warnings are the UX pattern to copy.

## Cross-cutting lessons for a competitor (Makeborne)

1. **Pick the layout model per output, not globally.** Use elastic/fluid blocks for web pages and review links, but render decks and book pages on a *fixed canvas from the start*. Gamma's entire export problem is a late conversion from fluid to fixed; a fixed-canvas internal model with overflow detection (what Gamma 5 now bolts on as an "automatic check") avoids it.
2. **Treat PPTX as a first-class render target with a layout-mapping layer.** Native shapes for text, tables and charts; embed heading/body/bold font files; declare upfront which effects degrade (gradient text, frosted glass, rounded tables) and avoid them in the deck theme by default. Editable-text rate is the metric reviewers now measure (Gamma ~30%, Beautiful.ai ~85%, Plus ~95%).
3. **Separate three things the market conflates:** a *theme* (tokens: fonts, colours, radii, image keywords), a *template* (content and layout patterns), and *content density* (brief/medium/detailed; card count vs text amount). Pitch's insight that brand = spacing, block structure and image style, not just hex codes, is the one to copy.
4. **Make variety a controlled parameter.** The "AI smell" is three-equal-boxes repetition. Constrain each deck to a layout rotation rule (no two consecutive slides with the same block structure) and vary hierarchy, not just colour.
5. **Illustration consistency = a persistent character/style asset, not a prompt.** Store a style object (palette, medium, line weight, reference images) and per-character reference sheets at brand level so decks, sites and books reuse them. Default to illustration over photoreal for people.
6. **Never render text inside generated images.** Typeset all copy in the layout layer; it is the only way to get embedded fonts, safe zones and editable translations.
7. **Books are arithmetic first.** Implement KDP's tables as code: three-edge bleed, gutter by page count, spine = pages × paper factor, even pages ≥24, 300 DPI at final size, PDF/X-1a with embedded fonts and flattened transparency, Kindle fixed-layout for picture books. Validate before export (Vellum's "Ideal Size" warnings; Inkfluence's crop/softness warnings) rather than letting KDP reject.
8. **Generate the cover wrap from the final interior**, not in parallel: spine width depends on page count, so cover export must be blocked until the interior is frozen. No general tool does this; Book Bolt does for low-content only.
9. **Build the AI-disclosure flag into the book object** and auto-populate it from provenance (which pages/images were AI-generated). KDP treats heavily edited AI output as still "AI-generated."
10. **Analytics must follow the share link, including published sites.** Gamma's published sites are untracked and Pitch's per-link analytics require consent; a client review link should capture viewer identity (email gate like Pitch), per-slide/page time, and tie back to the CRM contact.
11. **Approval is missing everywhere as a client-facing state.** Canva gates download/publish internally; nobody offers approve/request-changes per deck/page/book with history visible to the client. That plus a white-label portal is open ground.
12. **Don't lock users in without an exit.** Tome's users lost everything because there was no PPTX export and decks were deleted at shutdown; export completeness is a trust signal buyers now check.
13. **Context beats layout for "serious work."** Tome's founders concluded the missing ingredient was customer data, and Pitch/Gamma/Canva all shipped CRM and transcript connectors in 2026. A built-in client CRM that feeds briefs, names and logos into generation is a structural advantage, not a bolt-on.
14. **Credits and model defaults are a trust issue.** Gamma's lowest ratings come from silent defaulting to pricier models and bot support; show model choice and cost per generation.
15. **Do per-element and batch styling from day one.** "Styling applies per slide only" is a top Gamma complaint four years in; theme changes should propagate, with per-page overrides tracked.
